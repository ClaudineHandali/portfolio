Insights Analyst Intern

UC Office of the President

Jun 2026 – Present

I came in to analyze employee satisfaction surveys. I ended up questioning what the scores were actually telling us.

I noticed that a low rating didn't always mean an agent had done something wrong. Sometimes it was a policy they couldn't control. Sometimes it was routing — a customer would open a case with one agent, call back, get a different agent, and only the second agent would receive the survey.

So I started looking more closely at the survey questions and talking with supervisors about what they were seeing in the actual cases. The recommendations I made weren't just about changing the survey. They were also about how we interpret the scores in the first place.

Now I'm looking at whether different teams naturally receive different ratings because of the work they handle. If expedited teams consistently get higher ratings because customers are getting faster service, for example, then using the same satisfaction threshold to evaluate every team may not tell us much about individual performance.

AI Developer Intern

Service Agent

Mar – Jul 2025

I thought building an AI agent that calls people would mostly be about getting the model to work. It wasn't.

I built the system end-to-end using GPT-4, Twilio, n8n, and Supabase. During testing, I noticed something that wasn't obvious from the code: the AI kept talking over people. A short pause was enough for it to assume someone was finished, so conversations felt awkward and rushed.

I ended up engineering pause detection so the system would wait two seconds before responding. I also added checks to tell whether a real person had answered instead of another AI, and logic to end calls when someone picked up but didn't say anything.

Once those pieces were in place, the conversations felt much more natural. I also rebuilt the data pipelines feeding the CRM and worked on algorithms to improve the outreach strategy.

Python GPT-4 Twilio n8n Supabase CRM

Things I Didn't Expect

Working with customer satisfaction data made me realize that sometimes the bigger question isn't whether a number is high or low. It's whether the number is actually measuring what we think it is.

Understanding satisfaction data meant talking to the people behind the cases. A score wasn't just a number — it was a story about policies, routing, handoffs, and things completely outside anyone's control.

Building an AI calling agent taught me otherwise. Getting the model to work was one thing. Engineering when it should listen, wait, and speak was another. A two-second pause detection system ended up mattering more than the model itself.

Working with large datasets changed that. The cleaning is the interesting work more often than I expected.

Energy data, AI agents, climate visualization, computer vision, research infrastructure, customer analytics. None of it planned. I just kept following the problems I found interesting.
